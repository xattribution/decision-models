# Synthetic set: structure ladders

Goal: teach a small model to judge a bullet's structure on its own, with the content held fixed. For each seed bullet you write six versions of one accomplishment that differ only in structure, from 5 (best) down to 0.

## Facts

Read the seed and pull out its accomplishment: what the member did, how or what it involved, the numbers, the unit or level, and why it mattered. If the seed is weak or has no real accomplishment (praise, a duty), invent one plausible accomplishment in the same career area, with specific numbers. Every version of the group must carry the same facts and the same numbers, only arranged differently; levels 1 and 0 may drop or bury the "why" because that is what makes them weak. Don't add hype words, rankings, pushes or banned content (WAPS or test scores, PME attendance, CFC). Use [NAME] if a person is needed. Vary career fields and phrasing across groups; don't reuse the same verbs and templates.

## The six levels

- 5: the full shape, fitted: "- [strong past-tense action]; [how or what]--[why it mattered / result]", one accomplishment, parts in order, punctuation doing its job, at most 115 characters. Clip with normal shorthand to fit (f/, w/, mbrs, msn, sq, wg, prsnl, trng, coord'd, dvlp'd, ID'd, spt, tm, hrs and approved acronyms), the way Space Force and Air Force bullets are really written.
- 4: the full shape but a little loose: slightly wordy or a weaker separator, 100 to 130 characters, still one accomplishment in order.
- 3: the shape is there but weak: two accomplishments crammed in with slashes, or a "why" that only restates the action, or 130 to 180 characters.
- 2: the parts out of order (result first, action buried), or one run-on sentence with no separators, or over 180 characters.
- 1: little shape: a list of duties in present tense, or a trait with the numbers attached, or "action--promote" with nothing between.
- 0: no bullet shape: a narrative paragraph of two or more full sentences, or a pile of fragments.

Levels must be clearly separated: a careful reader shown the six in random order should put them back in order.

Clipping is neutral and must not mark the level. In each group pick at random whether the group leans clipped or spelled out, and mix within it: about half of level 4 versions use the same shorthand as level 5, some level 5 versions spell words out when they still fit 115 characters, and levels 3, 2 and 1 use shorthand about as often as not. What makes level 4 weaker is looser phrasing (a filler phrase, a weaker separator such as ", which" or ", resulting in"), never spelling words out. Separators must not mark the level either: about half of level 4 versions keep the "--" and are weaker only through filler or a wordy part, and some level 2 and 3 versions keep "--" too. Don't let length rank the levels either: a short level 1 or 2 and a long level 4 are fine. Rotate the forms each level allows across groups (for level 3: slashes, a restated why, or length; for level 2: out of order, run-on, or too long; for level 1: duty list, trait plus numbers, or action--promote; for level 0: narrative or fragments) so no one form becomes the only sign of a level.

## Output

Tab-separated lines, no header, six lines per seed, in any order:

seed id	level	text

No tabs or line breaks inside a text. Write nothing else.
